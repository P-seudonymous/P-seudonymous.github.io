My areas of interest/expertise are the research derivatives of Hardware Security(HWSec), which would be Side Channel Attacks(SCA) and Fault Injection attacks(FIA) coupled with logic design.

> Hardware security is a discipline originated from the cryptographic engineering and involves hardware design, access control, secure multi-party computation, secure key storage, ensuring code authenticity, measures to ensure that the supply chain that built the product is secure among other things.
>
> -[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_security)

If we look at HWSec, it becomes obvious that you do not need _only_ hardware knowledge to understand what is going on. It is to be a jack of quite a few trades, which can be statistics, cryptography, deep learning, systems etc.

The endgoal should never be to understand hardware security, but to understand one particular domain and then work your way through implementation security.
For me, I am intrigued equally by cryptographic math and firmware security, but I have explored more of the cryptography side until now.

Over the past year, I have worked independently, due to a lack of a team or like minded people, but that has probably made me explore domains more in depth, since you need to have more of a grasp of the entire project, than you would have to, working in a team.
